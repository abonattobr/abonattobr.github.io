Short Biography

Associate Professor at the Federal University of Health Sciences of Porto Alegre (UFCSPA), he teaches in the Medical Physics program and coordinates the Beam Physics Group (GFF). A permanent faculty member of the Graduate Program in Information Technologies and Health Management (PPGTIGSaúde), he is also an accredited co-advisor in the Nuclear Technology Program at IPEN/USP. He earned his BSc in Physics (2005) and his MSc (2008) and PhD (2013) in Sciences from the Federal University of Rio Grande do Sul (UFRGS), with an emphasis on Theoretical Physics. Postdoctoral appointments included the Federal University of Paraná (2013), focusing on laser-pulse propagation in plasmas; Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (BELLA, 2014–2015), where he studied compact deceleration of relativistic electron beams using plasma beam dumps; and the University of Manchester in collaboration with the Cockcroft Institute (2019–2020), joining the Novel Accelerator Group to investigate beam acceleration and deceleration in plasmas via wakefields driven by laser pulses or electron beams.


His work spans beam, laser, and plasma physics, with a focus on laser–plasma interactions and particle acceleration. Current research targets plasma-based compact accelerators and radiation sources for medical applications. Topics include electron acceleration in gaseous plasmas and in nanostructured solid targets based on carbon nanotubes or graphene layers, coherent synchrotron radiation generation, very-high-energy electron (VHEE) radiotherapy, FLASH radiotherapy, and radioisotope production via photoactivation. Machine-learning approaches — particularly Bayesian optimization — are employed to optimize accelerators for these applications.


He is a founding member of the NanoAc collaboration, contributing to a multi-institutional network involving institutions from Spain, the United Kingdom, Italy, Greece, and Brazil, focused on developing ultracompact electron accelerators based on laser-driven wakefields in nanostructured solid targets. In Brazil, he collaborates with IPEN/USP within the LIRA project, which aims to implement the first relativistic-intensity laser in the country. Membership includes the Brazilian Physical Society (SBF), the American Physical Society (APS), and the International Committee on Ultra-High Intensity Lasers (ICUIL).
